Two different feelings

Output: suspenseThe roll is the big moment. Hope, cheers and groans. But a bad roll can erase a good plan, and players blame the dice.
Input: a puzzleThe dice set the problem; the player solves it. More control and less blame, but less drama at the table.

Many games mix both: roll dice to decide your options, then roll once more to see if the big move works.

What designers take from this

  1. Output randomness (decide, then roll) creates suspense. The roll is the climax, and failure can ruin a plan.
  2. Input randomness (roll, then decide) creates a puzzle. The roll sets your options, and skill decides how well you use them.
  3. Order is a design choice. The same dice gave 4 points one way and 7 the other. Pick the order that matches the feeling you want.
You'll see this in: Kingsburg, Risk, Roll for the Galaxy, Talisman.
