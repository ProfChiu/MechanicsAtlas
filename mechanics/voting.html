Secret ballotsDee broke a promise and nobody could prove it.Hidden votes turn deals into trust games.

Every voter asked the same two questions: "Does this help me?" and "Does this help the leader?"

What designers take from this

  1. A majority is a price. To pass anything, a player must share the gain with enough others. Votes become a currency.
  2. The table protects itself. Nobody votes to let the leader win, so voting games pull leaders back on their own.
  3. Open or secret is a dial. Open votes keep promises honest; secret votes add bluffing and betrayal.
You'll see this in: Twilight Imperium, Secret Hitler, Junta, The Resistance.
