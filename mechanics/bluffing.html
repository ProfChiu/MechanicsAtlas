Twelve dice on the table. Every one of them is hidden under a cup.
Players roll by shaking the cup, then slam it down.
You can see: 3 dice
Hidden from you: 9 dice
Every player is in the same spot. Nobody knows the full count.
A bid sounds like this:
"At least two "
It counts every die on the table: yours, theirs, all twelve.
You may claim things you cannot see. That is the whole point.
After "two 4s", the next bid could be:
✔ two 5s (higher face)
✔ three 2s (more dice)
✘ two 3s (that is lower)
Or, instead of bidding, say:
"LIAR!"

What designers take from this

  • Hidden information creates the game. Lift every cup and there is nothing left to claim or doubt.
  • A bluff must look like a truth. Claims near the expected count are hard to call. Wild claims invite a challenge.
  • Symmetric penalties keep both sides risky. The liar pays when caught and the caller pays when wrong, so nobody bids or calls for free.
You'll see this in: Liar's Dice, Coup, Skull, Cockroach Poker.
