A designer dial: are deals binding?

Binding

  • An agreed deal is a rule. It must happen.
  • Trust is easy, so players trade more and plan further ahead.
  • Betrayal is impossible. The drama comes from the terms.

Non-binding

  • A deal is only a promise. It can be broken.
  • Trust becomes a resource that players earn and spend.
  • Betrayal is part of the game, and so are grudges.
Many games sit in between: trades made now are binding, promises about later turns are not.

What designers take from this

  1. A trade needs two winners. Ada and Bo both scored from one swap, which is why the swap happened.
  2. The table protects itself. Vera offered a great price and nobody took it, because helping the leader helps no one else.
  3. Binding or not is a dial. Enforced deals build trust; broken promises build drama. Pick the one your game wants.
You'll see this in: Catan, Diplomacy, Bohnanza, Chinatown.
