Take-That

Cards that hurt someone else

Press SPACE to start

Point the attacks up

Cards that say "steal from the leader" or "from anyone ahead of you" aim the catch-up for the players.

FIXES: picking on the weak

Never knock anyone out

A floor (you always keep 1 coin) or a quick way back in. Nobody sits and watches.

FIXES: player elimination

Hide or blur the score

If nobody is sure who leads, it is hard to hand someone the win on purpose.

FIXES: kingmaking

Make attacks cost you

Playing a card uses your turn. Hurting others should slow you down a little too.

FIXES: endless pile-ons

What designers take from this

  • Players aim at the leader, so take-that is a built-in catch-up system. In our game the gap shrank from 5 to 2 in three cards.
  • The same freedom causes kingmaking: a player who cannot win can still pick who does.
  • Targets matter. Shape them with rules (hit the leader, no elimination, hidden scores) so attacks feel fair.
You'll see this in: Munchkin, Exploding Kittens, Unstable Unicorns, Love Letter.
