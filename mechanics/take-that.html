How designers keep take-that fair

Limit the targetsAttack only the leader, or only the player to your left.Stops dogpiles and grudges.
Give a defenceShield or counter cards let a target fight back.Being attacked becomes a decision, not just bad news.
Hurt resources, not playersSteal points or cards; never knock anyone out of the game.Everyone keeps playing to the end.
Keep it shortA 15-minute game shrugs off a bad round.Long games make attacks feel personal.

What designers take from this

  1. Attacks flow to the leader. Three players hit Dee in one round and the gap shrank from 7 to 2. Take-that is a built-in catch-up system.
  2. Targeting is the real decision. Who you hit, and when, matters more than which card you hold.
  3. Manage the risks. Dogpiles, kingmaking and player elimination can sour a game. Limit targets, offer defences, and keep everyone playing.
