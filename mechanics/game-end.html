Many games add one more rule: finish the round once the end triggers, so every player gets the same number of turns.

What designers take from this

  1. A known end invites planning. With fixed rounds, players saved up for a big last turn, and the final round decided the winner.
  2. A player-triggered end invites racing. First to 15 rewarded speed; a slower, stronger plan never got its turn.
  3. A shared clock invites a tug of war. When the supply ends the game, the leader rushes it and everyone else tries to slow it down.
You'll see this in: Agricola, Splendor, Ticket to Ride, Carcassonne.
