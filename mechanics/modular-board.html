Same seven tiles, three different games

With a fixed board, the best start is the same every time and players stop exploring it. With modular tiles, every game asks a new question: where is the best spot this time?

What designers take from this

  1. A fixed board gets solved. When the best start never moves, players memorise it and the opening stops being a decision.
  2. Modular tiles keep openings fresh. Shuffling the same seven tiles moved the best spot every game. Players must read the board, not remember it.
  3. Exploration adds discovery. Flipping tiles mid-game reveals rewards and dangers that change plans. Balance the tile mix so no single flip decides the game.
You'll see this in: Catan, Betrayal at House on the Hill, Mage Knight, Tikal.
